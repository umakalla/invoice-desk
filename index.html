      What this does

      It takes one vendor invoice, reads it, runs the checks an AP clerk would run, and ends with one of three decisions. Every decision comes with its reasons in plain words, so anyone can see why.

      ApprovedEvery check passed. It is booked against the PO and scheduled for payment on the vendor’s terms.
      Needs reviewSomething a person has to judge. It goes to the review queue with the question already written out.
      Returned to vendorSomething only the vendor can fix. A draft email says exactly what is needed.

      The eight steps

      1. Receive. Note the file and take a fingerprint of it, so the exact same file is caught if it comes in twice.
      2. Read. If the PDF has real text, read it directly. If it is a scan, there is no text, so it goes to AI reading.
      3. Pull out the fields. Rules look for each field by its usual labels (“Bill No.”, “Invoice #”, “Inv No”). Line items are found as rows where quantity × rate = amount, whatever the layout. Our own PO format is searched for anywhere on the page.
      4. Check the invoice itself. Required fields are there, the numbers add up, and GST is a real rate, the right type for the vendor’s state, and the right amount.
      5. Check the vendor. On the approved list, not blocked, GSTIN valid and matching our records, bank account matching our records.
      6. Check for duplicates. Against every invoice already booked: same number once punctuation and leading zeros are ignored, or same amount and date under a different number.
      7. Match to the PO. Each item against the PO line, price against PO price, quantity against what is still left on the PO after earlier invoices.
      8. Decide. Anything the vendor must fix returns it. Anything we must judge holds it. Otherwise it is approved.

      The edge cases, and why these four

      1. One PO split across invoices (2a, then 2b)

      Kaveri ordered 10,000 cartons on one PO and delivered in two lots. Invoice 2a bills 6,000 and is approved. Invoice 2b bills 5,000, but only 4,000 are left. The process remembers what earlier runs used up, holds 2b, and suggests approving 4,000 and asking for a credit note. A check that looks at one invoice at a time would pay both.

      2. A re-sent invoice with a new-looking number (3)

      Apex re-sends invoice AIE-0912, already paid, as “AIE/912” with a payment reminder. A plain text match misses it. The process compares numbers with punctuation and leading zeros removed, finds the paid original and returns it.

      3. No PO number at all (4)

      Nilgiri, a small transporter, writes a letter-style invoice: no PO, no item table, GST included in one total. The process works the tax back out, scores this vendor’s open POs on amount, wording and date, and suggests the best one. It does not pay on a guess: a reviewer confirms in one click.

      4. A scanned paper invoice (5)

      There is no text to read, so AI reads the image. AI can misread a digit, so nothing it reads is trusted on its own: quantity × rate must equal each line, lines must add to the subtotal, tax must match its rate, and everything must match the PO. One wrong digit breaks at least one of these and the invoice is held. When AI isn’t available, the scan goes to manual keying instead of guessing.

      Two more worth running

      6. Coromandel bills 1.2% above the PO price. That is within tolerance, so it is approved and the difference is noted for procurement. 7. Sharma Steel’s invoice says “our bank account has changed”. Everything else is perfect, but payment is blocked until someone calls Sharma on the number already on file.

      Choices I made

      • Rules first, AI second. Rules are instant, free and explain themselves. AI is used only for scans, or when rules miss a required field, and its output still has to pass every check.
      • Return versus hold. If only the vendor can fix it, return it with a clear note. If we need to judge it, hold it with the question written down.
      • Held invoices don’t use up the PO. Only approved ones do, so a held invoice can’t block the next one by mistake.
      • Every check runs and shows its result, even after a problem is found, so the reviewer sees the whole picture in one place.

      Assumptions

      • Invoices arrive as files. In production, an email inbox watcher would feed this same process.
      • The vendor list, POs and invoice register would come from the ERP. Here they are a fixed test dataset (see Vendors and POs).
      • Indian GST rules; the buyer is in Tamil Nadu (state code 33). Amounts are in rupees.
      • Matching is invoice against PO (two-way). Goods-receipt matching is the next step.
      • Each stage shows its real processing time. The screen paces stages slightly so a person can follow along.

      What I would build next

      • Read invoices straight from the AP inbox.
      • Three-way match: also check that the goods were actually received.
      • Partial approval in one click (approve 4,000 of 5,000 and request a credit note).
      • Post approved invoices to the ERP, and export the audit trail.
